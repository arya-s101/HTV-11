## Product and differentiation to test
Answer: “Given my income, housing cap and destination, which Toronto areas meet **both** my housing and commute limits, and what must change if none do?” This is a **personal Toronto affordability boundary**, not a generic rental score. Its value must come from a defensible decision using local evidence. It is not proven unique in the market.

User enters household income and monthly/annual + gross/net basis, housing cap, bedroom requirement, household size, destination, commute mode/limit, weekday/arrival or departure time, commute days/month and preference for money versus time.

Return a feasible set of sourced Toronto areas, plus areas close to the boundary. For every area, show dated rent benchmark and its actual zone/geographic scope, commute minutes, benchmark-to-housing-cap gap, commute-to-limit gap, rent burden, modeled transport dollars, and why it passes or fails. If none pass, say so plainly and show the **minimum housing-cap increase and/or commute-limit relaxation for each near match**, with units and source uncertainty. First prove ONE result. Then three areas, no-match behavior and a constraint change that visibly changes the feasible set. Manual address/rent/utilities analysis is secondary.

Benchmark feasibility is a **screening estimate**, not proof a unit exists at that price. A rent-only threshold cannot certify total housing spend when utilities and fees are unknown. Mark it provisional and show unknowns. Show monetary housing + commute total separately from time; do not turn commute minutes into money via an arbitrary hourly wage. Two TTC options with the same trip frequency will often have the same fare cost. Never engineer the demo around transport-dollar savings that are absent.

**No live inventory dependency.** No authorized feed established. Rentals.ca/Condos.ca restrict scraping; Facebook Marketplace is especially unsuitable. Do not scrape or build hidden endpoint aggregators. Neighbourhood recommendations are the primary product, not an embarrassing fallback. No imaginary available apartments disguised as listings.

## Calculation rules that must survive implementation
CAD integer cents. Annual/12 monthly income. Explicit gross/net label. Gross income does not permit a take-home cash claim. Rent/income burden and shelter/income burden distinguish rent vs utilities. Zero/missing income produces unavailable ratio, never infinity.

Housing cost includes known separately payable utilities/fees; unknown stays unknown or editable labelled assumption. Inspect rent definition to avoid double-counting included utilities. Total = housing + modeled transport, label **estimated housing + commute total**, not full household budget. Food/debt/childcare/etc excluded unless provided.

TTC adult eligible PRESTO/contactless checked at$3.30, two-hour transfer,47fare monthly cap with consistent payment method. Recheck official fare page when implementing. Model paid travel periods/days and other monthly paid trips. Don't count bus legs as separate fares. TTC candidate costs may be equal; no forced dollar savings from shorter trip. No GO in first slice.

Compute housing gap = benchmark housing amount minus stated cap and commute gap = trip minutes minus stated limit. A positive gap is a shortfall; a negative gap is headroom. Keep exact amounts/minutes visible. Filter known constraints, then order feasible areas by the user's stated money/time preference. No result meets both? Say so, then separate near matches and show each violated limit plus the change needed. Unknown required route/rent cannot pass or score zero. Time remains minutes, not secretly monetized. Context demographics don't determine an individual's suitability. Do not add an overall 0–100 score or scam/trust score to create artificial distinctiveness.
