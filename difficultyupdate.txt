# PRD — Toronto Rent Decisions Game

> Working title. Rename after the core loop works. **Do not use "RentShield"** (taken by [rentshield.ca](https://rentshield.ca/), a related product). Don't buy a domain until availability and event-code terms are checked.

**Context:** Hack the Valley 11 — [2026 School of Cities challenge](https://hack-the-valley-11.devpost.com/): civic tech for a real Toronto problem tied to the **October 26, 2026 municipal election** ([City election page](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/elections/)), founded on Toronto urban data.

---

## 1. One-line summary

A short, playable 8-bit life sim: the player creates a pixel-art character and guides them through life stages (student, first job, new job, moving in with a partner, kids, retirement), making a Toronto rental decision at each stage. Each decision reveals evidence-backed tradeoffs and what they didn't know, then asks the player to separate personal actions from city-level levers. Across the run they collect civic questions and leave with **one sourced, nonpartisan question to ask a municipal candidate**.

**Core loop (runs once per life stage):** choice → consequence → evidence → who has power → question.
**Meta loop (across stages):** each later stage shows whether the player's reasoning improved — the life progression replaces a separate replay.

**10-second pitch:** "Pick a Toronto rental. See the budget and commute tradeoffs you missed. Then learn which parts of the problem your next city council can actually change, and leave with one evidence-backed question to ask candidates."

## 2. User and goals

**Primary user:** a Toronto renter or prospective renter making a housing decision who wants its civic context before the election.

**Goals**
- Increase decision literacy (spotting unknowns, separating cash from time).
- Increase civic literacy (correctly attributing a constraint to a municipal lever).
- Produce a candidate question that depends on the player's own choice and cited evidence.

**Non-goals — the app MUST NOT**
- Promise to find an available home or show real listings.
- Determine legal rights or give legal advice.
- Tell people how to vote, endorse candidates, or invent candidate positions.
- Predict a policy's dollar impact.
- Claim to be "first" or "only", or to have invented a genre.
- Claim it changes votes, saves money, reduces emissions or improves housing access.

If the answer is "compare rent and commute", go back to ideation. ## 4. Scope — first playable scenario (MVP)

Civic lever for scenario 1: **TTC service only.** The City lists the [TTC as an agency managing Toronto transit](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/accountability-operations-customer-service/city-administration/city-managers-office/agencies-corporations/agencies/), and [TTC service standards are used in route and service planning](https://secure.toronto.ca/council/agenda-item.do?item=2025.TTS2.1). Housing planning comes later, once its lever is equally well sourced.

### Character creation

- **8-bit character creator:** layered original pixel-art sprite (hair, skin tone, clothing, accessory, colour palette). All sprites original — no recognizable game characters or brand styles.
- **Customizable age:** the player sets a starting age; the game starts at the life stage that age falls into and the character ages through later stages. Default 20.
- **Appearance is cosmetic only.** It never changes income, offers, outcomes or feedback.
- **Demo preset button** loads a fixed character + starting age so the judged demo is reproducible.

### Life stages

Each stage is one full run of the flow below, with its own illustrative household, income, destination and civic lever. Ages are defaults; stages can be skipped.

| # | Stage | Default age | Housing need | Civic lever | Lever source status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Student | 20 | Room/shared unit near campus | TTC service | Sourced (§4 links) |
| 2 | First job | 23 | First solo rental (hero demo, §4 values) | TTC service | Sourced |
| 3 | New job | 27 | Move for a new workplace | Housing near transit / planning | To verify |
| 4 | Moving in with a partner | 30 | Larger unit for two | Affordable-rental delivery ([City housing plan](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2023/ex/bgrd/backgroundfile-234815.pdf)) | Partially sourced |
| 5 | Kids | 34 | Family-sized unit | Family-sized units in planning; childcare as a shared-jurisdiction example | To verify |
| 6 | Retirement | 66 | Accessible / downsized home | Seniors' housing and accessibility; rent rules as a non-municipal example | To verify |

**A stage ships only once its lever is backed by an official source.** Stages marked "to verify" stay hidden or show a "coming soon" card until then.

Life-event rules:
- Partner of any gender; kids optional; any stage can be skipped. No stage or choice is "correct" or rewarded for being traditional.
- Life events change household size, destination and illustrative income — never appearance-based.
- **All stages use today's dated figures, labelled "in 2026 dollars".** The game never projects future rents, fares or incomes.

### Carry-over between stages

- **Civic notebook:** each stage adds the player's drafted question; the exit artifact is the one they pick from the notebook.
- **Reasoning tracker:** per stage, record whether the player flagged unknowns before choosing and attributed the lever correctly. Show the trend across stages (this is the learning-outcome evidence).
- Optional light stats (e.g. monthly cash left, hours commuting) carry across stages for flavour, but no score for spending more or less, or for commute length.

### Difficulty

Chosen at the start; changeable between stages. Difficulty changes **how information is presented, never what is true.**

| Level | What the player sees before choosing |
|---|---|
| Easy (default, demo) | All known costs as plain line items; unknowns explicitly flagged as "unknown"; commute shown in minutes |
| Normal | Some costs only appear in listing text ("+ hydro", "utilities extra"); the player must spot and flag them |
| Hard | Key facts must be deciphered: costs hidden in lease-style fine print or abbreviations, commute given only as stop/route info, and the benchmark given as a raw CMHC survey-zone table the player must read |

Rules:
- Every hidden fact is recoverable from what's on screen — hard means "needs decoding", never "missing" or "misleading".
- Provenance badges (`measured` / `illustrative` / `unknown`) stay visible at every level.
- The consequence reveal shows everything plainly at every level, plus what the player decoded vs. missed.
- Scoring rewards decoding and flagging unknowns; higher difficulty can multiply that score, never punish a cash-vs-time preference.

### Negotiation mode (LLM landlord)

After choosing an offer, the player can optionally negotiate with the landlord in a chat, before signing.

- **The LLM plays the landlord's voice; code decides the deal.** Each offer has a deterministic, hidden negotiation config: asking rent, floor rent (lowest acceptable), allowed concessions (e.g. utilities included, move-in date, parking), and which arguments move the landlord (e.g. citing the CMHC benchmark, flexible move-in, longer stay).
- The LLM returns structured output (`message`, `proposed_rent`, `proposed_concessions`, `deal_status`). Code validates it: any rent below the floor or concession outside the allowed list is clamped/rejected. The final rent is labelled **"illustrative — negotiated"**.
- **Evidence as leverage:** arguments that use the stage's real evidence (e.g. the dated CMHC benchmark) move the landlord more than generic haggling. This ties negotiation back to the evidence step.
- Difficulty sets the floor's distance from asking, the number of concessions, how many turns the landlord tolerates, and how much evidence-based arguments help. Hard landlords may cite "other applicants" or refuse to budge on price but trade a concession.
- Walking away is a valid outcome; the player returns to the offer list.
- **The landlord never states legal rights or rules** (deposits, rent increases, eviction). Ontario tenancy law is provincial and outside this app's scope; if the player asks, the landlord deflects and the UI links an official Ontario source.
- Prompt-injection proof: player messages can't change the floor, because the floor isn't in the model's power to override. Landlord stays in character, stays respectful, and ends the chat on abusive input.
- **Scripted fallback landlord** (deterministic dialogue tree) runs if the API fails, times out, or for the demo preset.
- Negotiation is optional and never required to reach the civic turn.

### Flow (per stage)

1. **Start** — show the character, their age, and the stage's illustrative circumstances (income, household size, destination, transit preference).
2. **Choose** — 2–3 fictional offers, each labelled **"Illustrative scenario, not an available listing."** Before choosing, the player sees: listed rent, utilities (amount or explicit *unknown*), neighbourhood/survey zone, one-way commute estimate with its status. The player can mark items they'd verify before signing. Options must genuinely trade off cash, time and missing information — no "correct" home.
2b. **Negotiate (optional)** — chat with the landlord about the chosen offer (see Negotiation mode); accept a deal or walk away.
3. **Consequence** — show separately: monthly listed housing cash, rent burden vs. a stated income basis, TTC fare under a dated rule, commute time. Reveal one unknown (e.g. utilities). **Never convert time to money by default.**
4. **Evidence reveal** — at least two Toronto-specific, labelled facts that feed the explanation or question (see §6).
5. **Civic turn** — prompt: *"What would you verify yourself, and what public decision affects this constraint for many renters?"* Player picks one personal action and one municipal question from 2–3 options each, sorting into: personal verification step, municipal issue, non-municipal/shared-jurisdiction issue. Feedback cites the source and explains any jurisdiction error. Link the [City housing plan](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2023/ex/bgrd/backgroundfile-234815.pdf) or relevant record.
6. **Question** — one neutral, specific candidate question built from the player's choice + cited evidence (not a canned string shown to everyone), saved to the civic notebook. Label as a draft question, not an endorsement or predicted outcome.
7. **Next stage** — age the character, show the reasoning tracker. Don't reward spending more or shame a longer commute.

**End of life / exit artifact** — summary of the character's homes, the reasoning trend, and the notebook. The player picks one question to copy, with a link to the [official election hub](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/elections/).

### Hero demo values — Stage 2, First job (all illustrative)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Persona gross income | $55,000/yr → $4,583.33/mo |
| Trips | 20 workdays, 40 one-way journeys/mo |
| Offer A | $1,600 rent, 60 min one-way → burden ≈ 34.9% |
| Offer B | $1,750 rent, 30 min one-way → burden ≈ 38.2% |
| Time difference | 30 min × 40 = **20 hours/mo** |
| TTC fare (both) | 40 × $3.30 = **$132** ([Sept 2026 adult fare](https://www.ttc.ca/news/2026/September/Back-to-school-on-the-TTC)) |

B costs $150 more in listed rent and saves 20 h of scheduled travel; **no fare savings**. Either preference is reasonable. Real cost can vary with two-hour transfers, other trips, discounts and the [47-paid-trip monthly cap](https://www.ttc.ca/riding-the-ttc/Updates/Monthly-fare-capping-coming-to-the-TTC). Commute times stay labelled illustrative until a validated router produces them.

Example question shape: *"In areas where renters face long commutes, what measurable TTC service action would you prioritize, and when would residents see a public progress report?"*

## 5. Hard rules (for any implementation)

- **MUST** keep fictional persona/offers in separate data from real Toronto observations.
- **MUST** label every displayed number with its status: `measured`, `derived`, `illustrative`, or `unknown`.
- **MUST** compute all figures deterministically in code. AI/LLMs **must not** create costs, figures or geography joins.
- **MUST NOT** invent fare savings from a shorter trip.
- **MUST NOT** imply a councillor can personally change a route, promise a schedule, lower market rent, or that City Hall can unilaterally set rents / instantly add supply.
- **MUST NOT** present CMHC averages as live asking rents, or GTFS as a computed route time.
- **MUST NOT** depend on Facebook Marketplace or any unlicensed listing feed.
- RentSafeTO data appears only with a verified real-building match, and never as proof a unit is available or safe.
- Any score rewards evidence use and recognizing uncertainty — never income or neighbourhood chosen.
- **MUST NOT** let character appearance affect any number, offer or feedback.
- **MUST NOT** project future prices; every stage uses dated 2026 figures, labelled as such.
- **MUST NOT** ship a life stage whose civic lever lacks an official source.
- **MUST NOT** let difficulty remove provenance labels or make a fact unrecoverable.
- **MUST NOT** accept an LLM-proposed rent or concession without code validation against the offer's negotiation config.
- **MUST NOT** let the LLM landlord give legal information or advice.
- **MUST NOT** expose the LLM API key in client code.

## 6. Data

Use a small static scenario file. Every field carries provenance:

```ts
type Field<T> = {
  value: T | null;          // null when status === "unknown"
  unit: string;             // "CAD/month", "minutes", "%"
  geography: string;        // "Fictional", "CMHC Toronto survey zone X", "Neighbourhood 158-model #N"
  reference_period: string; // "2021 Census", "Oct 2025 survey", "Sept 2026 fare"
  source_url: string | null;
  status: "measured" | "derived" | "illustrative" | "unknown";
};
```

### Candidate real sources (dated context only)

| Source | Use | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| City of Toronto 158 neighbourhood boundaries (Open Data) | Map/locate offers by neighbourhood | — |
| 2021 Neighbourhood Profiles (Open Data) | Renter / shelter-cost-burden indicator | Census-derived, 2021, neighbourhood geography |
| CMHC Rental Market Survey, Toronto workbook | Survey-zone rent benchmark | Survey-zone averages, **not** live asking rents; different geography/year than profiles — label both |
| Apartment Building Evaluation (RentSafeTO) | Only with verified building match | Evaluates participating buildings, not units |
| TTC GTFS static feed | Future routing | Planned service only; **no route computed yet** |
| TTC fare pages (links above) | Fare rule | Dated; note transfers and cap |

Sampled sources must be re-fetched/verified in this repo; record exact URL, fields and retrieval date in `data/SOURCES.md`.

## 7. Suggested technical shape (default — change if the team prefers)

- Static single-page web app, no backend required for MVP.
- `src/character/` — layered pixel-art sprite creator (canvas or CSS grid at integer scaling, `image-rendering: pixelated`), age picker, demo preset. Character state in memory.
- `data/stages.json` — per stage: id, default age, household, illustrative income, destination, offers, lever id, `enabled` flag (false until lever is sourced).
- `data/evidence.json` — real observations with provenance.
- `data/levers.json` — civic levers with jurisdiction, explanation and source URL.
- `src/calc/` — pure functions: monthly income, rent burden, fare cost (with cap), time difference. Unit-tested against §4 values.
- `src/question/` — builds the exit question from player choice + selected lever + cited evidence (template-based; deterministic).
- UI components per flow step; a persistent "illustrative / measured" badge on every number.

## 8. Acceptance criteria (MVP)

**Build order:** Tier 1 = character creator + Stages 1–2 + end screen. Tier 2 = remaining stages, each only once its lever is sourced. Don't start Tier 2 until Tier 1 passes everything below.

- [ ] Character creator works: at least 4 customizable sprite layers, starting age picker, demo preset.
- [ ] Starting age maps to the correct starting stage.
- [ ] Changing appearance changes no numbers (test).
- [ ] Demo preset plays character creation → two stages → exit question in under ~4 minutes, no narration needed.
- [ ] Civic notebook collects one question per stage; end screen lets the player pick and copy one.
- [ ] Reasoning tracker shows per-stage results for unknowns flagged and lever attribution.
- [ ] Every offer shows the "Illustrative scenario, not an available listing" label.
- [ ] Calc tests pass: $4,583.33/mo; 34.9% / 38.2%; 20 h; $132 for both offers; cap logic at 47 paid trips.
- [ ] Every number on screen shows status + source link (or "illustrative"/"unknown").
- [ ] At least one unknown is revealed after the choice.
- [ ] At least two labelled Toronto facts appear in the evidence step, each with geography and reference period.
- [ ] Civic turn gives jurisdiction feedback with a source link on wrong attribution.
- [ ] Exit question changes with the player's choice/lever (not identical for all players), names a lever and a measurable outcome, and links the City election hub.
- [ ] No text endorses a candidate, predicts a policy outcome, or claims "first/only".
- [ ] Copy-to-clipboard for the question.

**Stretch:** Tier 2 stages; pixel-art scene per stage (campus, office, family home); chiptune sound.
